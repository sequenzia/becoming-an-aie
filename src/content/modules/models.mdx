## Transfer connection

This is the Phase 1 fixture draft of the Models module. Its prose is placeholder text that exercises the module layout, the artifact card, the workshop and failure forms, and the self-check. Phase 2 replaces every paragraph. The frontmatter, the structure, and the self-check questions are real.

This module expands one row of the talk's transfer table: testing discipline becomes eval discipline. What stays the same: the habit of checking before accepting a result, and the refusal to ship on a green run you did not read. What changes: the unit of measurement. A test checks one path you wrote. An eval measures a model's behavior across a population of your own cases, and that population is the evidence behind every selection and every replacement.

### Select, measure, replace

Fixture draft. When you are the owner, the model is a component you select, measure, and replace. Select on six axes: capability on your tasks, cost per completed task, latency at p95, context window, tool-use reliability, and data residency. Measure your product on representative cases under its operating conditions, because public benchmarks use a task population and a harness you did not choose. Replace with one of two strategies: a pinned version, which gives you controlled migration and lifecycle management, or a moving alias, which gives you automatic updates and regression monitoring. A snapshot controls one source of variation. Prompts, tools, retrieval, and the environment also affect behavior.

## Failure exercise

<FailureExercise>

<Artifact id="models-fixture-trace" />

Fixture exercise. Something in this trace will cause a migration with no evidence behind it. Find it and explain it in a few sentences.

<Fragment slot="explanation">

The request model is a hardcoded id in a config file, the vendor has started returning a deprecation header with a retirement date, and the harness has no eval suite and no last eval run. When the retirement date arrives, the team will choose a replacement it has never measured. The fix is not a different model id. It is an eval suite over representative cases, run against the current model today and against the replacement before the switch.

</Fragment>

</FailureExercise>
